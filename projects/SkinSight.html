Skin-conformal array imaging

Abstract

Array-based imaging systems offer unparalleled capabilities for mapping complex environments, but their inherent structural rigidity prevents intimate integration with dynamic, non-rigid hosts such as human skin and soft robots. While flexible visual sensor arrays offer compliance, fabrication constraints severely limit their pixel density and overall resolution. Here, we present SkinSight, a skin-conformal imaging system that overcomes this trade-off through a hybrid “rigid-in-soft” architecture. By integrating miniaturized imagers with stretchable interconnects, SkinSight achieves stable, high-resolution imaging under complex mechanical deformations. Furthermore, to resolve processing challenges induced by continuous deformation, we leverage large-scale pre-trained vision transformers for end-to-end geometric inference. This deformation-adaptive framework no longer relies on rigid structural priors, enabling robust, calibration-free 3D visual perception. We demonstrate seamless integration with human skin, transforming a human palm into a sensory interface capable of perceiving objects beyond the direct line of sight. By augmenting the perceptual capabilities of humans and robots, SkinSight demonstrates broad potential for next-generation wearables and embodied artificial intelligence.

Rigid-In-Soft Architecture and Reconstruction

SkinSight physically decouples optical sensing from mechanical strain: each rigid node integrates a CMOS image sensor, micro-lens array, lens holder, and nano-lens into a compact imager module, while the surrounding soft matrix and serpentine interconnects accommodate bending, twisting, and stretching. Because deformation creates a continuously changing, non-rigid camera geometry, SkinSight pairs this hardware with an asynchronous computational framework that estimates view poses and 3D point maps from temporally chunked video streams, then refines the scene through Gaussian optimization. By leveraging pre-trained vision transformers for end-to-end geometric inference, the framework avoids rigid structural priors and supports robust reconstruction without pre-calibrated camera extrinsics.

Augmented Vision From the Palm

When attached to a human palm, SkinSight transforms the hand into an active sensory interface. The palm-mounted array captures views from inside an occluded space, reconstructs objects beyond the user's direct line of sight, and aligns the reconstruction with augmented-reality glasses. Beyond human augmentation, the same skin-conformal visual capability points toward soft robotic systems that can perceive while remaining compliant.
