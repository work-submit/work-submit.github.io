Abstract

Array-based imaging systems can map complex environments, but their structural rigidity makes them difficult to integrate with deformable hosts such as human skin and soft robots. SkinSight addresses this gap through a hybrid rigid-in-soft architecture that combines miniaturized CMOS imagers, stretchable interconnects, and a soft substrate. The system maintains stable, high-resolution imaging during bending, twisting, and stretching. To handle continuous deformation, it uses large-scale pretrained vision transformers for end-to-end geometric inference, enabling robust, calibration-free 3D visual perception. We demonstrate SkinSight on human skin as an augmented sensory interface for perceiving occluded objects beyond the direct line of sight.

Rigid-in-soft Device Design

SkinSight shifts the sensing unit from individual stretchable pixels to compact imager modules. Each rigid node protects a high-density CMOS sensor, while serpentine copper interconnects and PDMS encapsulation absorb mechanical deformation across the soft substrate. This lets the array retain megapixel-level imaging capability while conforming to curved and moving surfaces.

Deformation-adaptive Reconstruction

The computational framework decouples fast online geometry recovery from asynchronous Gaussian optimization. Temporally chunked video streams provide camera poses and dense point maps, while the Gaussian pathway refines the scene into a continuous, photorealistic 3D representation. This avoids relying on fixed array geometry and supports reconstruction under dynamic deformation.

Augmented Vision on Human Skin

Integrated on the palm, SkinSight acts as a wearable visual interface that explores occluded spaces and registers reconstructed geometry into an augmented-reality view. Fast point-map rendering provides geometric feedback, while Gaussian rendering offers higher-fidelity visual immersion for objects beyond direct line of sight.
