FS-DVS: A Frequency-Selective Dynamic Visual Sensing Paradigm for Enhancing Information Completeness

Feiyu Ji1 Xiaokang Yang1 Xiaoyun Yuan1,
1MoE Key Lab of Artificial Intelligence, AI Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University
PDF arXiv

Abstract

Dynamic vision sensors (DVS) offer exceptional temporal resolution and dynamic range by asynchronously reporting pixel-level intensity changes. However, conventional DVS rely on a per-pixel independent triggering mechanism and therefore ignore the spatial integration performed by biological retinal ganglion cells. FS-DVS addresses this information bottleneck by placing a learnable spatial filter strictly before event triggering, enabling the sensor response to be driven by local spatio-temporal dynamics rather than isolated pixels. With a differentiable event simulation framework, this filter can be optimized end-to-end with downstream tasks. Starting from a delta-like initialization, the learned filters spontaneously evolve into center-surround patterns that emphasize mid-frequency components, align with human contrast sensitivity, and improve structural completeness with high noise resilience across detection, recognition, and segmentation.

Task-Adaptive Pre-Trigger Event Generation

FS-DVS transforms event generation from a fixed pixel-wise detection process into a task-adaptive perceptual encoding mechanism. In simulation, input frames are first processed by a single learnable spatial filter, then passed through a frozen differentiable event simulator and downstream network so task gradients can update the filter. For physical validation, the learned filter is applied to high-frame-rate videos before display and real event-camera recapture, allowing the same sensing prior to be evaluated under hardware noise and non-ideal sensor behavior.

Structural Completeness in Simulated and Real Events

FS-DVS shows consistent superiority over the conventional single-pixel DVS baseline in both differentiable simulation and physical sensor-in-the-loop validation. In object detection, performance peaks with a 7 × 7 learned filter, reaching a +12.3 mAP gain in simulation and maintaining a +10.8 mAP margin in the physical domain. Qualitative results reveal the mechanism behind these gains: conventional DVS often produces sparse and fragmented responses for kinematically subtle targets, while FS-DVS preserves more complete contours for distant vehicles, pedestrians, and scene structures.

Center-Surround Filters and CSF-Like Selectivity

Although detection and recognition impose different downstream objectives, their optimized filters consistently converge toward an antagonistic center-surround topology. This structure mirrors ON-center/OFF-surround retinal ganglion cells, whose lateral inhibition enhances local contrast and extracts stable edge primitives. In the frequency domain, the same learned filters exhibit ring-shaped energy distributions, revealing band-pass responses concentrated in the intermediate-frequency range.

Bandwidth Efficiency and Hardware Feasibility

The learned filters spontaneously rediscover a biological band-pass trend: they suppress low-frequency illumination redundancy and high-frequency stochastic noise while focusing on informative intermediate-frequency structure. Under matched event rates, FS-DVS continues to improve detection performance, whereas conventional DVS collapses at high sensitivity because lowering thresholds mainly introduces random noise. Mutual-information analysis further supports this interpretation, with FS-DVS increasing recoverable information from 0.98 to 1.06 bits per pixel on average.
