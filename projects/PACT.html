Massively parallel functional photoacoustic computed tomography of the human brain

Shuai Na* Jonathan J. Russin* Li Lin* Xiaoyun Yuan* Peng Hu Kay B. Jann Lirong Yan Konstantin Maslov Junhui Shi Danny J. Wang Charles Y. Liu✉ Lihong V. Wang✉
Nature Biomedical Engineering 2022
Paper

Overview

Blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) functional magnetic resonance imaging of the human brain requires bulky equipment for the generation of magnetic fields. Photoacoustic computed tomography obviates the need for magnetic fields by using light and sound to measure deoxyhaemoglobin and oxyhaemoglobin concentrations to then quantify oxygen saturation and blood volumes. Here, we show that massively parallel ultrasonic transducers arranged hemispherically around the human head can produce tomographic images of the brain with a 10-cm-diameter field of view and spatial and temporal resolutions of 350 micrometres and 2 seconds, respectively. A comparison of functional photoacoustic computed tomography and 7 T BOLD functional magnetic resonance imaging showed a strong spatial correspondence in the same field of view and a high temporal correlation between BOLD signals and photoacoustic signals, with the latter enabling faster detection of functional activation. These findings establish the use of photoacoustic computed tomography for human brain imaging.

Brain Function and Angiographic Structures

Empowered with high spatiotemporal resolution and sensitivity, the 1K3D-fPACT can image both brain function and angiographic structures, facilitating coregistration between fPACT and fMRI results. The 1K3D-fPACT angiography and magnetic resonance angiography result in strong visuospatial correlation. Quantitatively, the measured vasculature diameters of participant 1 exhibit strong agreement, highlighting the potential of 1K3D-fPACT for diagnosing cerebral vascular diseases.
