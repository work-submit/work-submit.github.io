Overview

Photoacoustic computed tomography can lose focus when heterogeneous tissue perturbs acoustic time of flight. PAGS addresses this problem without calibrated speed-of-sound priors by combining sparse Gaussian photoacoustic sources with a compact continuous propagation field. Instead of optimizing a dense voxel-wise medium or an independent value for every source–transducer path, PAGS places spherical-harmonic probes on a sparsely sampled 3D voxel grid and recovers the anisotropic path-averaged speed of sound continuously through spatial and angular interpolation. This parameter-efficient field directly corrects arrival-time alignment while the source and propagation parameters are jointly optimized from measured signals.

Speed-of-Sound-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting

The core of PAGS is a sparse probe representation of a continuous anisotropic path-averaged speed-of-sound field. Each grid probe stores only nine second-order spherical-harmonic coefficients. For every Gaussian source–transducer path, PAGS first interpolates coefficients from the eight neighboring probes and then evaluates their directional spherical-harmonic response. In a representative setting with 100K sources and 4.6K transducers, a direct path table would require 460M learnable values; a 16 × 16 × 16 probe grid requires only 36.9K coefficients—about 12,500× fewer—while remaining independent of source splitting, pruning, and motion.

Autofocusing in Simulation and Physical Measurements

On heterogeneous in-silico data, PAGS reconstructs sharper, more continuous vasculature than uniform-speed UBP and vanilla SlingBAG, reaching 30.3 dB PSNR and 0.537 SSIM. On the physical phantom, the learned propagation correction improves local contrast and separates nearby absorbers more clearly, without requiring the tissue–water boundary used by the prior-informed Dual-SoS baseline.

3D Results and Sparse-View Robustness

The supplementary video presents rotating 3D comparisons of vanilla UBP, Dual-SoS UBP, SlingBAG, and PAGS, followed by PAGS reconstructions with progressively fewer detector channels. Major vascular trunks and branch connectivity remain visible down to 12.5% sampling. The sparse Gaussian source prior and smoothly interpolated low-order spherical-harmonic field together keep the reconstruction stable as the number of measurements decreases.
