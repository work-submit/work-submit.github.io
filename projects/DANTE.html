Training large-scale optoelectronic neural networks with dual-neuron optical-artificial learning

1Department of Electronic Engineering, Tsinghua University 2BNRist, Tsinghua University 3Institute for Brain and Cognitive Science, Tsinghua University 4Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School

Abstract

Optoelectronic neural networks (ONNs) are a promising avenue in AI computing due to their potential for parallelization, power efficiency, and speed. However, training large-scale diffractive networks faces challenges due to the computational and memory costs of optical diffraction modeling. Here, we present DANTE, a dual-neuron optical-artificial learning architecture. Optical neurons model the optical diffraction, while artificial neurons approximate the intensive optical-diffraction computations with lightweight functions. DANTE improves convergence by employing iterative global artificial-learning steps and local optical-learning steps, enabling large-scale ONNs that were previously difficult to train.

Dual-Neuron Optical-Artificial Learning

Existing single-neuron learning approaches connect all optical neurons into one large differentiable function and optimize the entire physical system directly using backpropagation. In DANTE, the dual-neuron switch alternates between two steps. In the global artificial-learning step, artificial-neuron layers are connected as a complex-valued ANN and trained to fit the dataset. In the local optical-learning step, the network is decomposed into independent layer pairs, and each optical-neuron layer is optimized to align its impulse response with the corresponding artificial-neuron layer.

Training Speed and Large-Scale ONNs

In simulation experiments, DANTE achieves a training acceleration of approximately 200 times and elevates accuracy by approximately 10% on the CIFAR-10 benchmark compared to single-neuron learning. DANTE also enables the training of large-scale ONNs with 150 million neurons on ImageNet, achieving performance on par with the representative VGG network in simulation.

Physical Optoelectronic Demonstration

We further develop a two-layer physical ONN system based on DANTE. The implemented system uses off-the-shelf optical modulation devices and electronic relay operations, and can effectively extract features to improve the classification of natural images. The results provide evidence that DANTE can be successfully implemented on physical ONNs despite assembly errors and system noise.
