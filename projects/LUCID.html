Abstract

Photography is the art of painting with light, yet nighttime scenes are shaped by competing degradations: intense flares obscure scene structure, while photon-limited regions collapse into noise. Conventional approaches address these factors in isolation, overlooking the fact that these degradations are fundamentally entangled. To bridge this gap, we introduce LUCID, a unified framework that reframes nighttime restoration as a continuous and controllable process rather than a fixed correction. LUCID enables users to restore challenging nighttime images with flexible control over exposure, light sources, flare, and ghosting artifacts, while also supporting high dynamic range (HDR) reconstruction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LUCID consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across diverse real-world nighttime scenarios.

The released checkpoints are available on Hugging Face, including the main LUCID restoration model and the flare disentanglement network used for inference.

Continuous Exposure Control

LUCID provides continuous exposure control for nighttime restoration, allowing users to smoothly adjust output brightness while maintaining structural consistency and natural appearance across challenging scenes.

Representative Results

Holistic Nighttime Restoration. These examples cover four diverse nighttime regimes from ExDark. LUCID restores structure and visibility more consistently across these challenging cases.

Flare Mitigation. These examples focus on removing scattering artifacts while preserving plausible light-source appearance. LUCID achieves a cleaner balance between removal and preservation than prior flare-removal baselines.

Single-Image HDR Reconstruction. LUCID extends naturally to HDR reconstruction through controllable exposure synthesis. These examples show stronger structural fidelity and more natural luminance than specialized HDR baselines.

Downstream Creative Applications. LUCID can be used both before and after downstream editing or generation. It removes interfering flare while preserving controllable exposure and lighting behavior.
